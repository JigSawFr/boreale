# The website

Boréale's website, <https://boreale.dev/>: the projects, what they share, what they do not do, in the consoles' fifteen languages, as PlayGuard's, PlayShelf's and Aurore's sites: English at the root, each other one in its folder (`/fr/`, `/fr-ca/`, `/de/`, `/es/`, `/es-419/`, `/it/`, `/nl/`, `/pt/`, `/pt-br/`, `/ru/`, `/ja/`, `/ko/`, `/zh-hans/`, `/zh-hant/`); one dark look (the brand stands on the night, `branding/README.md`).

A first visit to the root goes to the browser's language when the site has it (Canada's French, Latin America's Spanish, Brazil's Portuguese and Traditional Chinese told apart by the browser's region); the header's language button opens a menu of the fifteen, each with its flag (`languages()` in `tools/build_site.py`, a popover in the look of the menu of Boréale's sites), which leads to the same page in another language, and the choice is kept (`assets/lang.js`, `boreale-lang` in the browser's storage, the language's folder). The flags are drawn by `assets/fonts/twemoji-flags.woff2` (4.6 KB, the fifteen cut out of Twemoji's, CC-BY 4.0, `LICENSE-twemoji-flags.txt`), so that every system shows them, Windows and Chromium on Linux included.

## How it is made

`tools/build_site.py <out-dir>` (Python 3, nothing to install) writes the pages:

- `template.html`: the page around the content (head, header, footer). Its aurora (`hero.html` under it) is `canvas#sky`, drawn by `assets/site.js` at 30 frames a second, and not at all while it is scrolled out of view; without script, or with less motion asked, `assets/site.css`'s gradient or a still frame stands in. The name opens a menu of Boréale's sites (`SITES`: boreale.dev, the homebrew, the libraries, then the open data and protocols, Atlas and Lantern), each project with its latest release, its own leading home (`switcher()` in `tools/build_site.py`, a popover the browser closes on Esc or a click elsewhere): its CSS and script are the "site switcher" blocks of `site.css` and `site.js`, copied as they are into PlayGuard's, PlayShelf's and Aurore's sites, this one the reference.
- `home.html` and `hero.html`: the home page, each project's card with a capture of it (`assets/shots/`), a link to its simulator or demo and one to its code, then, lighter and without a capture, the open data and protocols: Atlas (its explorer) and Lantern (its specification), each with its code (`ATLAS`, `LANTERN` in `tools/build_site.py`; the footer links both too); `legal.html`: `legal/`, the privacy and legal notice (what the site counts, who publishes it, who hosts it), written in French: each other language's says under its lede that it is a translation and that the French text prevails, with a link to it (`legal_translation`, which `fr.json` alone has not); `lost.html`: `404.html` in each language, kept out of search engines. GitHub Pages serves the root's at any address; it sends a visitor to another language's when the address is in its folder (`/de/...`), or it was chosen, or the browser prefers it (`assets/lang.js`).
- `support.html`: `support/`, how to donate (GitHub Sponsors, monthly or once; Ko-fi, once, a goal per device) and the hardware the donations buy to test on, each with its state (`hw_next`, `hw_wanted`, `hw_later`; the next goal lit), what is already here, and what a donation is not (a gift: no feature, support or early build in return). It is linked from the header (but on a phone narrower than 520px, where it no longer fits beside Projects), from a section of the home page and from the footer; `legal/` says what the platforms pass on (`privacy_5`).
- `insights.html`: `insights/`, a pointer to Atlas's own site, <https://atlas.boreale.dev/> (`ATLAS` in `tools/build_site.py`), where PlayInsights' data now lives with its explorer, kept so that its old links still lead somewhere.
- **The home page's showcase** (site.css's and site.js's "showcase" blocks, the same in PlayGuard's, PlayShelf's and Aurore's sites, adapted to each one's markup): the night going on down the page (veils of aurora, faint stars, a horizon over the footer); the projects' cards, larger, their parts on the same rows (a subgrid), lifting under the pointer, their edge lit where it is and the project's mark glowing; the latest releases as a timeline (still a list: the dates beside a line of aurora, a dot on it for each release); the sections rising into view once; a short cross-fade from one page to the next where the browser can. Without script, or with less motion asked, everything stays in place.
- `i18n/<language>.json`: each `{{key}}`'s text, one catalog per language, fifteen (`en.json` the reference; `fr-CA.json`, `es-419.json`, `pt-BR.json`, `zh-Hans.json`, `zh-Hant.json` by the apps' codes). The names of screens and features are the apps' own (PlayGuard's, PlayShelf's and Aurore's catalogs), the menu of Boréale's sites' texts PlayGuard's site's word for word; each language's typography (French spaces before `:` and, in France, `;` `?` `!`; «» in Russian; „“ in German; CJK punctuation). Chinese, Japanese and Korean lines break as each script wants (site.css: Japanese between phrases, Korean between words, Chinese never before a punctuation mark, its titles where the catalog's `<wbr>` says). The fonts have only Latin letters: the other scripts are drawn by the system's. A release's date is written as each language does (`MONTHS`, `DATES`); its lede, in English, is on the English pages only.
- The projects' links are in `build_site.py` (`PROJECTS`): PlayGuard at `guard.boreale.dev`, PlayShelf at `shelf.boreale.dev`, Aurore at `aurore.boreale.dev`, each in the same language folder as the page (the four sites share the fifteen folders), with their simulators in the page's language (`?lang=`, the apps' codes) and Aurore's demo in it where its menu has it (`DEMO_LANGUAGES`; else the browser's).
- `assets/shots/`: one capture per project, 960×540 WebP, each in its dark theme as the site is: PlayGuard's `images/screenshots/dashboard.png` and Aurore's `.github/readme/cadran.png` (its demo's Welcome tab) from their repositories, scaled down, PlayShelf's catalogue from its site (`assets/images/screenshots/en-US/catalogue-dark.webp` at shelf.boreale.dev, made by its `tools/site_screenshots.py`; the one in its repository is the light theme). Made-up games only.
- **The projects' releases**, read from GitHub's API at each build: the latest version on each card, the five latest releases on the home page, the last twenty in `feed.atom`, the latest version of each in `versions.json` (`{"schema": 1, "projects": {…}}`, which PlayGuard's, PlayShelf's and Aurore's sites read at their build, for their menu of Boréale's sites), each with its lede (the first paragraph of its notes that is only text; on the English pages only, the notes being in English); a project without a release says so on its card. `GITHUB_TOKEN` lifts the API's anonymous limit; `SITE_OFFLINE=1` builds without them (no version, no releases section); `SITE_STRICT=1` fails when they cannot be read rather than build without them (main's workflow).
- **For search engines**: schema.org data on each page (Boréale, its site, each project and its latest version), each page's languages (`hreflang`, in the pages and in `sitemap.xml`), `robots.txt`, the social preview with its locales; `manifest.webmanifest` and `favicon.ico` (written by `tools/export-branding.mjs`) for browsers.
- `assets/`: the CSS, the script, the marks, the icons, the social preview and the fonts (SIL Open Font License; the flags' CC-BY 4.0), served with the site so that no visitor's browser loads anything from anyone else.
- **The audience**, counted by Umami Cloud (<https://cloud.umami.is>): `assets/umami.js` is its tracker, served with the site, that sends each page seen to `gateway.umami.is`, without cookie, and only from `boreale.dev` (not from a preview or a local copy). It is a copy: take it again from <https://cloud.umami.is/script.js> when Umami changes it. A link to another of our sites (`guard.`, `shelf.`, `aurore.boreale.dev`…) says where the visit comes from, for Umami's *UTM* report: `build_site.py` (`utm()`) adds `utm_source=boreale` and `utm_medium=nav` (header), `footer` or `link` (the page's text); a link to boreale.dev itself or to anyone else carries none, and `check_site.py` checks both.
- A **Content-Security-Policy** on every page (a `<meta>`: GitHub Pages sends no headers): only the site's own files, no inline script or style; the one address it may send to is Umami's (`connect-src`).
- `.well-known/security.txt` (RFC 9116): a flaw is reported privately on GitHub; it expires a year after the month it was built in.
- `SITE_URL` is the site's address (`https://boreale.dev/` by default).
- `build.sha256`: a hash of everything built, for the workflow to tell whether a scheduled build has anything new.

## Checks

- `tools/check_site.py <site>`: the catalogs have the same keys, no `{{key}}` is left, every link to the site and every `#anchor` leads somewhere, nothing the Content-Security-Policy blocks is on a page, and the schema.org data, the feed and the manifest read.
- `tools/site_layout.py <site>` (Playwright for Python): every page on two phones, a phone in landscape, a tablet and a desktop: none wider than the screen, no target under 24px, no script that throws, and nothing axe-core finds against WCAG 2.2 AA (`SITE_AXE=<axe.min.js>`).

## Publishing

`.github/workflows/site.yml` builds and checks the site for each pull request and for main, and publishes main's on GitHub Pages. It runs again once a day, and on a `repository_dispatch` of type `release`, so the versions follow the projects' releases (those publish only when the site built differs from the one served, by `build.sha256`; on main, releases GitHub cannot give fail the build, and the site served stays): a project's release workflow can send one with a token allowed to (`gh api repos/JigSawFr/boreale/dispatches -f event_type=release`).

Once, in the repository's settings: *Pages › Build and deployment › Source: GitHub Actions*, then *Custom domain: boreale.dev* and *Enforce HTTPS*. At the registrar, the apex's `A` and `AAAA` records point to GitHub Pages, and `www` is a `CNAME` to `jigsawfr.github.io`.
